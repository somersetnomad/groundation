## What happened

Over Easter in April 1963, at Coral Gardens in St James near Montego Bay, a violent confrontation left several people dead, including Rastas, local people and police officers.

The state's response went far beyond the people involved. Police and soldiers rounded up Rastafari across Jamaica. Many were beaten, tortured and jailed without charge, and many had their locks forcibly cut. Survivors recall that the order came from the very top of government. The community calls the day **Bad Friday**.

## Decades of silence

For years the event was barely spoken about outside the community. Survivors carried the memory, and the 2011 documentary *Bad Friday: Rastafari After Coral Gardens*, by the anthropologists Deborah A. Thomas and John L. Jackson Jr, helped bring their testimony to a wider audience.

## The report and the apology

In 2015 Jamaica's Office of the Public Defender reported on the incident and found gross breaches of the constitutional rights of the Rastafari who were targeted.

In April 2017 Prime Minister Andrew Holness issued a formal apology on behalf of the government, 54 years after the events. It marked the start of a programme of reparations and restoration. A trust fund, administered with the Rastafari Coral Gardens Benevolent Society, has since paid out more than J$130 million to survivors. The same process led to the protection of Pinnacle, the first Rastafari commune, as a heritage site.

## Land, at last

On Good Friday, 3 April 2026, during the community's annual commemoration of the Coral Gardens atrocity, land titles for two lots in Albion, St James were presented to the Rastafari Coral Gardens Benevolent Society. The community has also put forward a 13-point list of recommendations to the government, and many say the work of redress is not finished.
