<div class="ital-grid">
<div class="ital reveal"><h2>Close to the earth</h2><p>Food as near to its natural state as possible. Fresh, local, unprocessed, and grown without chemicals where it can be.</p></div>
<div class="ital reveal"><h2>No pork, often no meat</h2><p>Pork is forbidden, drawing on Leviticus. Many Rastas avoid all meat; some eat small fish but not shellfish or scavengers.</p></div>
<div class="ital reveal"><h2>Salt and additives</h2><p>Many avoid refined salt, using sea salt, herbs and pepper instead. Preservatives and artificial colourings are avoided.</p></div>
<div class="ital reveal"><h2>The cooking itself</h2><p>Some cook in clay pots or over wood rather than in metal, and many avoid alcohol and tobacco. Strictness varies widely.</p></div>

<h2 class="h-mid">On the ital table</h2>
<div class="dish-list">
<div class="dish"><b>Ital stew</b><span>Pumpkin, yam, dasheen and greens simmered in coconut milk, often with spinners (small dumplings).</span></div>
<div class="dish"><b>Run-dung</b><span>Vegetables "run down" slowly in coconut milk until it reduces to a thick custard.</span></div>
<div class="dish"><b>Roast breadfruit</b><span>Roasted whole in the fire until the skin blackens, then split and eaten plain.</span></div>
<div class="dish"><b>Callaloo</b><span>Steamed leafy amaranth with onion, thyme and Scotch bonnet.</span></div>
<div class="dish"><b>Irish moss</b><span>A thick drink made from sea moss with spices, valued for strength.</span></div>
