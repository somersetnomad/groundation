Women were called queens or empresses, and in many houses were expected to cover their heads, dress modestly and defer to their kingman.

Some customs drew on Old Testament purity laws. In the stricter houses, women observed separation during menstruation. Critics within the movement have called these rules a "Babylon" import of patriarchy; others see them as sacred order and protection.

From the late 1970s, Rasta women began organising, publishing and preaching on their own terms. Empress Menen, Haile Selassie's wife, is widely held up as a model of equal royal partnership: the "Queen Omega" to his "King Alpha".

<div class="pull">Many sistren now insist that the "I" in I and I has always included them.</div>
